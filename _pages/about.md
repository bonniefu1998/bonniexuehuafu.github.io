I am Bonnie Xuehua Fu, a PhD researcher in Applied Linguistics at the University of Auckland, New Zealand. I have submitted my doctoral thesis, which investigates generative AI-assisted digital multimodal composing in EFL writing instruction.

My research examines how learners draw on linguistic, multimodal, and technological resources to compose and learn. I am particularly interested in how instructional design supports human–AI collaboration, learning transfer, and second language writing development.

## Research interests

- **Second language writing and digital multimodal composing:** composing processes, multimodal meaning-making, and writing development.
- **Generative AI in language education:** human–AI collaboration, learner regulation, and research-informed instructional design.
- **Learning transfer:** how learners mobilise knowledge and strategies across composing tasks, modes, and contexts.
- **Multilingual education:** translanguaging and language learning in multilingual and English-medium settings.

## Doctoral research

My doctoral research develops and investigates a Generative AI-assisted process-multimodal genre (GenAI-P-MG) pedagogical framework for EFL writing instruction.

The study examines learners’ multimodal composing processes, video quality, and task perceptions, alongside their performance in linguistically oriented writing. It connects questions about how learners compose with questions about what they learn through composing.

## From research to learning design

I am also Co-founder and Chief Product Officer (Learning Design) at **YNOVA**, where I lead research-informed learning design for bilingual AI tutoring tools.

My work translates second language learning research, writing pedagogy, and assessment principles into scaffolded learning activities, practice sequences, and feedback. Working with teachers, learners, and the technical team also informs my research questions about how AI-supported learning can be designed and evaluated in practice.

## Background

Before beginning my PhD, I worked as a Senior Research Assistant and Project Manager at the City University of Hong Kong, supporting school-based projects on English reading and writing in STEM subjects.

I hold an MA in English (Applied English Linguistics) from The Chinese University of Hong Kong and a BA in English from Fuzhou University.
